It's usually the first question people ask, and it deserves a straight answer: it depends on what you need found out. But "it depends" isn't useful on its own, so here's how pricing actually works — and how to keep it under control.

## How investigators typically bill

**Hourly plus expenses.** Surveillance and most field work are usually billed by the hour, per investigator, plus mileage and direct expenses. Surveillance often has a minimum block of hours, because getting into position and waiting for a subject takes time whether or not they leave the house.

**Flat fees.** Many locates, background investigations and records searches can be quoted as a flat fee, because the scope is predictable.

**Retainers.** Most agencies ask for a retainer up front, applied against hours and expenses. Unused funds are typically returned or credited according to your agreement — make sure that's in writing.

## What drives the cost

- **The goal.** "Is my spouse meeting someone after work on Thursdays?" is a narrower question than "What is my spouse doing?"
- **The subject's predictability.** Someone with a fixed schedule is easier and cheaper to observe than someone whose routine changes daily.
- **Number of investigators.** Some surveillance safely requires two investigators to avoid losing the subject or being noticed.
- **Travel and distance.** Cases far from the Phoenix metro add mileage and travel time.
- **Turnaround.** Rush work and nights or weekends can affect pricing.
- **Reporting needs.** A quick summary differs from a detailed report and video package prepared for your attorney.

## How to get the most from your budget

1. **Share what you already know.** Schedules, vehicles, addresses, recent photos — every detail you provide saves billable hours.
2. **Define the question.** The more specific the objective, the more efficiently we can answer it.
3. **Time it well.** If you know when something is likely to happen, surveillance can be targeted to that window.
4. **Start small.** A short, targeted investigation often tells you whether a bigger one is needed.

## What you should always get

A written agreement with the scope, rates, expenses and retainer terms; regular updates; and a clear report at the end. If anyone won't put pricing in writing, keep looking.
