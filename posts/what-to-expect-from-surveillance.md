Surveillance is the most requested private investigation service — and the most misunderstood. It isn't a movie stakeout. It's careful planning, patience and documentation.

## Before anyone goes into the field

A successful surveillance case starts with a good plan. We'll ask you for:

- **Recent photos** of the person
- **Vehicles** — make, model, color, plate if known
- **Addresses** — home, work, places they frequent
- **Schedule** — work hours, routines, days when something is likely to happen
- **Your objective** — what exactly you need to know

The more specific the question, the more efficiently it can be answered.

## In the field

Investigators position themselves lawfully — usually in a vehicle on a public street — and wait for the subject to move. That waiting is part of the job. When the subject leaves, investigators follow at a safe distance, documenting stops, contacts and activities.

Some cases call for two investigators, which makes it far less likely the subject is lost in traffic or notices a single vehicle behind them.

## What gets documented

- **Time-stamped video and photos** of relevant activity
- **A detailed log** of locations, times and observations
- **Context** — who the subject met, how long they stayed, what they did

## What surveillance can't do

Investigators can only record what is visible from places they're lawfully allowed to be. They don't enter private property, record private conversations or place trackers on vehicles. If something happens behind closed doors, surveillance can document comings and goings — not what happened inside.

## Not every day produces results

Sometimes the subject stays home. Sometimes they do exactly what they said they would. "Nothing unusual happened" is a real result, and an honest investigator will report it as such. That's why timing matters: targeting the days and hours when activity is most likely gets you the most value.

## The report

At the end, you receive a clear written report with supporting photos and video, organized so you can share it with your attorney if needed.
