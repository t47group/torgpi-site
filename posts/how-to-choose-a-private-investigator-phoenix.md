Hiring a private investigator is usually something people do once or twice in their lives, often during a stressful moment. Here's a simple way to separate professionals from people who just have a camera and a website.

## 1. Confirm the license

In Arizona, private investigation agencies are licensed by the Department of Public Safety. Ask for the agency license number and verify it. [Here's how licensing works.](/blog/how-to-verify-a-private-investigator-license-arizona/)

## 2. Ask about cases like yours

Surveillance, skip tracing, corporate investigations and custody cases each require different skills. Ask:

- How often do you handle this kind of case?
- What usually makes these cases succeed or fail?
- What would you need from me?

## 3. Listen to how they talk about the law

A good investigator will tell you what they *won't* do — record conversations they aren't part of, use GPS trackers on people, access private accounts. If someone promises those things, walk away. You're the one who'll pay for it later.

## 4. Get the plan in writing

You should receive a written agreement covering:

- The objective and scope
- Hourly rates or flat fees
- Expenses and mileage
- Retainer terms
- How and how often you'll get updates

## 5. Ask what the final product looks like

Will you get a written report? Time-stamped photos and video? Copies of records? Is it organized so your attorney can use it?

## 6. Pay attention to the first conversation

Did they listen? Did they explain things clearly? Did they tell you honestly when an investigation might *not* be worth it? The way an investigator treats you before you hire them is the best preview of how they'll treat your case.

## 7. Trust, but verify the trust signals

Reviews and testimonials are helpful, but make sure they're real and specific. A licensed agency with a physical address, a clear name and transparent pricing is a better bet than glossy promises.
