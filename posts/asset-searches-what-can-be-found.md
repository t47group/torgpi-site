Before filing a lawsuit, it's worth asking a hard question: if you win, can you actually collect? An asset search helps answer that before you spend money on litigation — and helps you collect after you win.

## What a lawful asset search can find

### Real property

Property ownership is generally public record. A search can identify homes, land and commercial property owned by a person or their business — in Arizona and other states — along with mortgages and liens.

### Business interests

Corporate filings can reveal companies where a person is an owner, member, manager or officer. Business assets often matter more than personal ones.

### Vehicles, vessels and aircraft

Registration records can show what someone owns — subject to privacy laws that restrict how some motor vehicle records may be accessed and used.

### Liens, judgments and bankruptcies

Other creditors' judgments and tax liens tell you who else is in line, and bankruptcy filings can change what's collectible altogether.

### Lifestyle indicators

Open-source research and field observation can show whether someone's lifestyle suggests assets that don't appear in their name.

## What requires legal process

**Bank account balances and financial records** are protected by federal law. They generally can't be obtained by an investigator directly; they're typically reached through discovery, subpoenas or post-judgment proceedings. An asset search often tells your attorney *where to aim* those tools.

Be skeptical of anyone who offers to get bank balances without a court order. That's usually pretexting — and it's illegal.

## When asset searches make sense

- **Before suing:** Is the defendant collectible?
- **After winning:** Where are the assets you can pursue?
- **During divorce:** Is a spouse hiding or moving money?
- **Before lending or partnering:** Can this person back up their promises?
