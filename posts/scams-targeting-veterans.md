Our founder is a U.S. Air Force veteran and co-founder of a veteran-focused nonprofit, so this topic is personal for us. Veterans are targeted by scammers precisely because they have steady benefits, a strong sense of trust and a willingness to help fellow service members.

Here are the schemes we see most often and how to protect yourself.

## Benefits "consultants" who charge to file claims

Help preparing and filing a VA disability claim is available **free** through accredited Veterans Service Organization representatives and state benefit counselors, such as those at the Arizona Department of Veterans' Services. Be cautious of anyone who wants an upfront fee to file an initial claim or "guarantees" a rating increase. Ask whether they are VA-accredited and check before you sign anything.

## Pension poaching

Older veterans and surviving spouses may be approached with offers to "restructure" their assets so they qualify for VA pension benefits. These schemes can involve expensive financial products, hidden fees and transfers that create problems with other benefits.

## Fake veteran charities

Scammers use patriotic names and imagery to collect donations that never reach veterans. Before giving, verify the organization's tax-exempt status and look up how donations are actually used.

## Romance and "fellow service member" scams

Scammers pose as deployed service members — or target veterans directly on dating and social sites — building trust before asking for money for travel, medical emergencies or "leave papers." Real service members don't need civilians to pay for leave.

## Government impersonation

Calls, texts or emails claiming to be from the VA and asking for your Social Security number, bank information or a payment to "keep your benefits" are red flags. Hang up and call the VA directly using a number you look up yourself.

## How to protect yourself

- **Slow down.** Pressure and urgency are the scammer's best tools.
- **Verify independently.** Look up phone numbers and organizations yourself.
- **Never pay to file an initial VA claim** without checking accreditation.
- **Talk to someone you trust** before sending money to anyone you haven't met in person.
- **Report it** to local law enforcement and the FTC, and contact your bank immediately if money was sent.

## If it already happened

Don't let embarrassment keep you quiet. Scammers count on that. An investigation can help document what happened, identify who may be behind it and organize evidence for law enforcement, your bank and an attorney.
