Most injured workers are exactly that — injured — and deserve every benefit the system provides. But fraudulent and exaggerated claims raise premiums, strain small businesses and make things harder for honest claimants.

Here are the patterns that commonly justify a closer look. None of them proves fraud on its own. They're reasons to ask questions — lawfully.

## Red flags around the injury

- **No witnesses** to an incident that happened in a busy area
- **Reported late**, especially after a weekend or holiday ("Monday morning claims")
- **Vague or changing descriptions** of how the injury happened
- **The injury doesn't match the job** or the described mechanism
- **Timing near a layoff, discipline or termination**

## Red flags during the claim

- **Hard to reach** at home during the day, or always "just missed"
- **Resists returning to work** even with light-duty options
- **Missed medical appointments** or doctor-shopping
- **Tips from co-workers** about side jobs, sports or physical activity
- **Social media posts** showing activities inconsistent with claimed limitations

## What an investigation looks like

A lawful claims investigation can include:

- **Activity checks** — documenting what the claimant does in public view, with time-stamped video
- **Background research** — prior claims, litigation, other employment and business filings
- **Social media and open-source research**
- **Witness canvasses and recorded statements**

What it doesn't include: entering private property, contacting the claimant under false pretenses, or recording conversations we're not part of. Evidence gathered the wrong way can sink an otherwise valid defense.

## Objectivity protects everyone

A good investigator reports what they see — including when a claimant's activity is entirely consistent with their injury. That objectivity is what makes the findings credible to adjusters, attorneys and judges.
